06 · Lane Craft

How a machine changes lanes

A lane change is the most negotiated maneuver in driving — five distinct steps, each with its own failure mode. This is exactly the mechanism running in both simulators above.

🧠

1 · Decide

The planner needs a reason: a slower leader, a route exit, a merging lane. It weighs time gained against risk added — most candidate lane changes are rejected.

In this simulator

Triggered only when the lead vehicle is meaningfully slower than cruise speed; otherwise the car simply follows.

📏

2 · Gap check

Side and rear-corner radars confirm a safe gap ahead and behind in the target lane — including fast vehicles still closing from behind.

In this simulator

The target lane must be clear 45% of the view ahead and 30% behind, so an approaching overtaker vetoes the move.

🟡

3 · Indicate & commit

Signal first. Predictability is a safety feature — other drivers cooperate with maneuvers they can anticipate.

In this simulator

The amber indicator blinks on the turning side through every maneuver.

🎯

4 · Steer under supervision

A smooth lateral profile moves the car over — while an independent emergency-braking layer stays armed the entire time, ready to override the plan.

In this simulator

The AEB supervisor runs every frame above the planner and can hard-brake mid-overtake.

👁

5 · Merge & verify

Before returning to a lane, the car verifies the corridor beside and behind it is genuinely clear — and holds position if not. Returning early causes the classic blind-spot side-swipe.

In this simulator

Watch for "SHOULDER CHECK · HOLDING LANE" — the car waits in the passing lane until home is clear.

07 · The Real World

How driverless driving works in the USA today

The United States is the largest live laboratory for autonomy — three very different philosophies operating side by side. Facts verified July 2026; this industry moves monthly, so check the sources below.

🚕

Waymo — the geofenced L4 model

Fully driverless paid rides — nobody in the driver's seat — across more than a dozen US metros, from Phoenix and San Francisco to Dallas, Miami, Denver and Las Vegas, with 200+ million autonomous miles logged and a target of 20+ cities and a million rides a week by end of 2026. The recipe: LiDAR + radar + cameras, HD maps, and a strictly bounded operating domain — outside the geofence, the car simply won't drive.

📷

Tesla — the supervised L2 model

"Full Self-Driving (Supervised)" works on nearly any US road using cameras and neural networks only — no LiDAR, no HD maps — but the human remains the legal driver, monitored by an in-cabin camera. Its separate Robotaxi service, launched in Austin in June 2025 and since extended to Dallas and Houston, began with onboard safety monitors that are gradually being removed. Two bets: scale vs. certainty.

🛸

Purpose-built & freight

Amazon's Zoox runs purpose-built pods — no steering wheel, no pedals, riders facing each other — publicly on the Las Vegas Strip since September 2025 and in San Francisco, with Austin and Miami next. Aurora operates driverless heavy trucks on Texas freight corridors — a friendlier domain than city chaos: mapped, structured, repetitive.

⚖️

The rulebook

There is no single US federal self-driving law. NHTSA oversees vehicle safety and mandates crash reporting for automated systems, while each state issues its own permits — California requires DMV permits plus CPUC approval for paid rides, and Texas began requiring state authorization for commercial driverless operation in 2026. Every incident is logged, investigated, and public.

08 · Responsible AI

First, do no harm

An autonomous system inherits the ethics of its designers. These principles are not add-ons — they are wired into the decision machine running above, and you can watch every one of them fire.

🛡

Protect life above all

The harm hierarchy is absolute: people first, then animals, then property, then the vehicle, then the schedule. Arrival time is never traded against a living thing.

Live in the sim

The car full-stops for a cow or a dog even where traffic law alone wouldn't demand it — and it brakes for a crossing pedestrian before they enter its lane, by predicting their path. Anything the sensors can see gets a guaranteed zero-contact outcome; a collision can only occur when you disable the sensors that would have seen it.

🕊

Never chase, never pressure

An AV must never use motion as a threat — no tailgating, no nudging pedestrians, no forcing gaps. Patience is a safety behaviour, not a weakness.

Live in the sim

Blocked on all sides, the car waits — "PATH BLOCKED · WAITING" — until the hazard moves of its own accord.

🌧

Degrade gracefully

When conditions worsen, a responsible system slows down, widens gaps, weights fusion toward weather-proof sensors — and if it can no longer guarantee safety, it executes a Minimal Risk Condition: pull over, stop, ask for help.

Live in the sim

Switch to Monsoon or Night: cruise speed caps at 78% and the ACC follow gap stretches — "CRUISING · WEATHER CAUTION".

⛑

Redundant guardianship

Safety must not depend on the planner being right. An independent AEB supervisor watches every frame and can override any decision — even a lane change already in progress.

Live in the sim

"🛑 AEB SUPERVISOR · HARD BRAKE" fires above whatever the planner was doing.

🔍

Honest limits

A responsible system knows — and declares — what it cannot see. Overclaiming capability is itself a harm; it teaches humans to trust a blind spot.

Live in the sim

The Real vs Simulated strip, per-sensor MISSED readouts, and honest "SENSOR GAP" collisions instead of magical saves.

🤝

Human authority

Below Level 3 the human's input always wins, instantly. And at every level, decisions must be loggable, auditable and explainable after the fact — a black box that harms and cannot explain is unacceptable.

Live in the sim

The verdict pill narrates every decision in plain language — a tiny model of explainability.
